ABSTRACT
The green transformation of mariculture is a crucial aspect for promoting the sustainable development of the 
marine environment. To address financial constraints such as the high financial pressure on local governments 
and the difficulty of traditional fishing enterprises in meeting green credit conditions due to technical resource 
limitations, the paper proposes a peer incentive mechanism. Then, this paper focuses on the impact of peer 
incentive policy on the green transition of the mariculture industry by constructing a four-party evolutionary 
game model among local governments, financial institutions, green mariculture enterprises and traditional 
mariculture enterprises. The main results are, (1) There exists a conflict between environmental regulation and 
green credit, and peer incentive policy can effectively mitigate the conflict relationship. (2) The peer incentive 
policy can accelerate the green transformation of the mariculture industry more effectively than incentive- 
oriented environmental regulation. (3) Both the allocation ratio of peer incentive levy and the efficiency of 
monitoring of green fishing enterprises to traditional fishing enterprises can regulate the synergistic effect among 
these policies.
1. Introduction
In the context of the depletion of global fisheries resources, mari -
culture is an important way to utilize marine fishery resources 
( Galparsoro et al., 2020 ; Alsaleh et al., 2024 ). Global marine and coastal 
aquaculture production increased by 64% in the 10 years to 2019. 
However, there are a series of problems, including the deposition of 
nitrogen species ( Maxey et al., 2023 ), coastal deposition of organic 
matter ( Kessouri et al., 2021 ), and eutrophication in marine fisheries 
aquaculture ( Wei et al., 2023 ). Furthermore, the aquaculture industry 
has been identified as a significant contributor to environmental 
degradation and human health concerns due to its crude mariculture 
practices ( Lin et al., 2022 ), which impedes the sustainable development 
of the global economy. At present, it has become a hot topic in the world 
to realize a green transformation of mariculture by coordinating the 
symbiotic relationship between mariculture and the ecological envi -
ronment ( Fu et al., 2022 ).
Green transformation of industry refers to the shift of enterprises 
towards environmental protection, energy conservation, low-carbon 
and sustainability. This transformation is achieved through technolog -
ical innovation and adjustments of production methods, aimed at 
reducing environmental impact and promoting the coordinated devel -
opment of the economy, society, and environment ( Hou et al., 2018 ; 
Karlsson and Hovelsrud, 2021 ). However, in the market economy, en -
terprises, as “ rational economic man ” , usually base their decision- 
making behavior on the cost-benefit decisions ( Vriend, 1996 ; Semmler 
et al., 2022 ). When enterprises face financial constraints, their willing -
ness to choose environmentally friendly governance is often low. Sieg -
rist et al. (2020) suggest that financial pressure continues to be the most 
important factor that enterprises are concerned about in the decision-

when funds were scarce, enterprises often prioritized governance mea -
sures that protect short-term profits, neglecting long-term environ -
mental impacts. Initial research and development for green farming 
techniques and equipment typically requires significant capital invest -
ment. In the long-term, Azar and Ciabuschi (2017) and Liu et al. (2020a)
argued that green innovation led to an innovation compensation effect. 
However, the process remained fraught with uncertainty ( Roper and

enterprises tend to view green innovation as a high-risk investment. 
This indicates that financial constraints limit enterprises ’ willingness to 
engage in green innovation, thereby hindering environmental protec -
tion efforts. A further deduction is that funding plays a critical role in 
promoting the green transformation of the mariculture industry. It is 
also essential in the green farming behaviors of fishery enterprises 
( Guillen et al., 2019 ; Knapp and Rubino, 2016 ). Inadequate funding may 
cause fishing enterprises to lack sufficient incentives to innovate. 
Consequently, this directly affects the process of green transformation of 
the mariculture industry. That is, financial constraint has become one of 
the key factors constraining the sustainable development of the industry.
According to current research, measures to promote green trans -
formation of industries mainly include penalty mechanisms and incen -
tive mechanisms ( Clinton and Steinberg, 2019 ; Farza et al., 2021 ; Chang 
et al., 2024 ). The penalty mechanism primarily applies external pressure 
to compel enterprises to engage in green technological innovation. Yu 
et al. (2023) and Gao et al. (2022) examined the evolutionary path of 
green behaviors in marine enterprises under different tax and penalty 
strategies within the scope of marine environmental governance. 
Although the penalty mechanism can force enterprises to engage in 
green innovation, Fehr and G ¨achter (2002) and Li et al. (2024) argued 
that penalties had no economic effects. Greife and Maume (2020) sug -
gested that punishment may even provoke vindictive social behavior. 
Incentive mechanisms alleviate the financial constraints of enterprises 
by providing them with policy support and financial subsidies, thereby 
encouraging them to adopt environmentally friendly governance. 
Currently, Government Regulation and Financial Support are the main 
sources of funding to promote the green transformation of industries 
( Kidwell et al., 2016 ; Huang et al., 2019 ; Yu et al., 2021 ). In terms of 
government regulation, the government provides technical support and 
financial subsidies to enterprises with good green performance. 
Although green subsidies have a certain positive significance, the 
shortage of subsidy funds and the short policy implementation cycle 
may have a negative effect on the green transformation of industries. For 
example, Zheng and Yu (2022) examined whether governments subsi -
dize marine carbon sink fisheries and concluded that increased gov -
ernment subsidies were not conducive to the development of carbon sink 
fisheries. 
ˇ
S ¯umakaris et al. (2021) suggested that government financial 
incentives were one of the determinants of enterprises ’ achievement of 
energy efficiency innovations, but there was currently a huge gap in 
subsidized funding. Petrovi ´c and Lobanov (2020) conducted an empir -
ical analysis of the impact of investment on industrial innovation, using 
16 OECD countries as the study sample. They argued that long-term 
investment is beneficial for industrial innovation, but there is contro -
versy over whether short-term investment can promote industrial 
innovation. A single source of subsidy funding also puts significant 
financial pressure on the government, and the lack of funding will affect 
the effectiveness of government regulation ( Shattock and Horvath, 
2019 ; Liu et al., 2020b ; Wang and Zhang, 2020 ). In terms of financial 
support, financial institutions ease the financing constraints of industrial 
transformation through the issuance of green credit. While green credit 
policies have promoted technological upgrading of enterprises, the 
bank-led financing system has some limitations in practice. Especially 
for highly polluting enterprises, it is often difficult for them to obtain 
green credit support due to the high costs and risks associated with green 
innovation ( Chai et al., 2022 ), which further increases the financing 
constraints in the green transition. For example, Pang et al. (2022)
concluded that green finance did not always have a positive impact on 
green technological innovation, but instead green finance discouraged 
green innovation in the short to medium term. Zhou and Zhang (2023)
suggested that green credit had a crowding-out effect on green tech -
nology innovation in SMEs by increasing competition for credit 
financing.
In summary, financial constraints are a major factor limiting the 
green transformation of industries. Although governments and financial 
institutions have mitigated financial shortfalls for some enterprises 
through incentive-based environmental regulations and green credits, 
there are still a significant number of enterprises, especially high- 
pollution firms, facing a funding gap for their green transformation. 
Some scholars have pointed out that the optimization of government and 
enterprise financing channels can alleviate the problem of industrial 
financial constraints and indirectly promote the implementation of 
external incentive policies ( Huang et al., 2021 ), but few scholars have 
conducted quantitative studies on the problem of financing sources. The 
core issue of this paper is to alleviate the financing constraints of 
mariculture industrial green transformation by designing a peer incen -
tive mechanism. This mechanism is based on the concept of “ non-pu -
nitive green incentive mechanism with multiple funding channels. ” 
Specifically, this study aims to address the following problems: 
(1) What is the relationship between existing incentive mechanism?
(2) Can peer-to-peer incentives promote green transition in 
mariculture?
(3) What are the key factors in peer incentives that can be changed to 
accelerate the green transition in mariculture?
Furthermore, the reasons for choosing the evolutionary game model 
in this paper are as follows: Firstly, considering that peer incentive is an 
incentive mechanism proposed based on theory and practice, and due to 
the lack of relevant statistical data, it is not feasible to use econometric 
models for this research. Therefore, this paper adopts evolutionary game 
theory and employs numerical simulation to explore the peer incentive 
effect in the green transformation of marine aquaculture, effectively 
overcoming the limitation of lacking statistical data support. Secondly, 
since the green transformation of marine aquaculture is a dynamic 
process, evolutionary game analysis, as a dynamic simulation method, 
can more effectively predict and explain the behavioral changes of each 
stakeholder at different stages, making the research findings more 
insightful and practical. Finally, the green transformation of marine 
aquaculture involves multiple stakeholders, including the governments, 
financial institutions, and fisheries enterprises. Within the framework of 
peer incentives, the study also needs to distinguish between green and 
traditional fisheries enterprises. The strategic choices of fisheries en -
terprises are often influenced by various factors, such as the strength of 
government environmental regulations and the loan interest rates set by 
financial institutions. Therefore, game theory serves as a powerful tool 
for analyzing the strategy choices of fisheries enterprises, providing 
important theoretical support for understanding their decision-making 
processes.
The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 provides a 
review of the relevant literature and presents the contributions of this 
paper; Section 3 is the theoretical foundation and model construction of 
the evolutionary game model; Section 4 derives the replicated dynamic 
equations of the evolutionary game model; Section 5 presents the results 
of the ESS analysis of the evolutionary game model; Section 6 performs a 
policy simulation and analyzes the impact of some potential factors on 
the policy implementation effect through numerical simulation 
methods; Section 7 presents case study; Section 8 reviews the results, 
offers policy implications, and notes the limitations of the paper.

2. Literature review
2.1. Factors affecting the green transition in mariculture
Existing literature primarily discusses the factors influencing the 
green transformation of the marine aquaculture industry from both in -
ternal and external perspectives. From an internal perspective, aqua -
culture technology and management models are key factors driving the 
green transformation. For example, Xiao et al. (2019) and Kori ˇcan et al. 
(2022) suggested that efficient recirculating aquaculture systems not 
only reduce costs but also decrease water pollution, thereby promoting 
green industry development. Yu and Han (2020) argued that good 
aquaculture practices and disease management also play an important 
role in the green transformation. At the same time, small and medium- 
sized aquaculture enterprises within the industry often face difficulties 
in bearing the high costs and risks associated with green transformation 
due to limitations in technology research and development and financial 
reserves ( Farmery et al., 2021 ; Zhang et al., 2022a ). From an external 
perspective, factors such as environmental regulations and market 
conditions have a significant impact on the decision-making behavior of 
aquaculture operators ( Liu et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024 ). For example, 
the choice experiment by Li et al. (2024) demonstrated that economic 
incentive policies can significantly encourage aquaculture operators to 
adopt environmentally friendly production methods. Therefore, 
regardless of internal or external factors, the green transformation of the 
marine aquaculture industry relies on sufficient financial support. 
Milewski and Smith (2019) , Robotham et al. (2019) , and Dong et al. 
(2023) , through evaluating the green development levels of marine 
aquaculture in different regions, all emphasized that economic support 
is a necessary means to ensure the long-term green and healthy devel -
opment of the industry.
2.2. Financial constraints and the green transformation of the marine 
industry
Undoubtedly, the issue of financial constraints is one of the main 
obstacles to the green transformation of the industry, and a lack of 
sufficient funds will make the green transformation extremely difficult. 
Existing research mainly focuses on the role of government subsidies 
and green finance in addressing financing constraints. On the one hand, 
government subsidies provide crucial economic support for the green 
transformation of the marine industry, incentivizing marine-related 
enterprises to adopt more environmentally friendly and sustainable 
production methods ( Wu et al., 2022 ; Wan et al., 2023 ). On the other 
hand, financial instruments such as blue loans ( Shan et al., 2023 ) and 
blue bonds ( Thompson, 2022 ) have a positive impact on promoting the 
sustainable development of the marine economy ( Shiiba et al., 2022 ). 
However, relying on a single source of funding also presents some 
challenges in promoting green transformation. Relying solely on gov -
ernment funds not only increases the financial burden on the govern -
ment ( Shattock and Horvath, 2019 ; 
ˇ
S ¯umakaris et al., 2021 ), but may 
also introduce rent-seeking risks ( Chai et al., 2022 ; Pang et al., 2022 ). 
Moreover, some scholars argued that the vulnerability of the marine 
industry made it difficult for the sector to obtain sufficient credit support 
( Kleih et al., 2013 ). In the marine aquaculture industry, especially for 
high-polluting enterprises, due to their extensive farming practices, they 
often face stricter credit assessments and higher credit thresholds 
( Huang et al., 2023 ), which exacerbates the financial constraints in their 
green transformation process. To address these issues, some scholars 
have started to explore incentive mechanisms for multiple funding 
channels. Tirumala and Upadhyay (2024) proposed that traditional 
financing methods have failed to effectively address the financial con -
straints related to coastal degradation and marine resource protection. 
They argued that investment and collaboration between similar enter -
prises could become a key approach to alleviating the problem of 
financial shortages.
In summary, first, while research on the green transformation of 
marine aquaculture has clarified the importance of economic support, 
there is a lack of sufficient explanation regarding specific strategies to 
overcome financial constraints. Second, although scholars generally 
believe that government subsidies and green finance can alleviate 
financial constraints in the green transformation of the marine industry 
to some extent, issues such as heavy government financial pressure and 
high loan thresholds for polluting enterprises still exist. Finally, while 
some scholars have proposed that the multi-funding channel incentive 
mechanism ” can serve as an important supplement to government 
subsidies and financial support, this view remains at the policy sug -
gestion level and lacks in-depth theoretical analysis and quantitative 
research. Therefore, the main contribution of this paper is as follows: on 
the one hand, based on theoretical and practical foundations, a policy 
framework for peer incentives is proposed, exploring a new “ multi- 
funding channel incentive mechanism ” ; on the other hand, based on 
evolutionary game theory, numerical simulation analysis is used to 
verify the feasibility and effectiveness of this mechanism in the green 
transformation of marine aquaculture. This not only provides a new 
theoretical perspective for solving the financial constraints in the marine 
aquaculture industry, but also offers practical policy references for how 
governments and financial institutions can collaborate to promote the 
green transformation of the marine aquaculture industry.
3. Theoretical foundation and model construction
3.1. Theoretical foundation
Local governments implement environmental regulatory policy, 
including “ financial subsidies ” and “ environmental taxes ” to promote 
the green transformation of the mariculture industry. In this case, sub -
sidies act as a positive incentive, while taxation serve a counter- 
incentive. However, government subsidies will increase the govern -
ment ’ s financial pressure and affect the government ’ s willingness to 
spend green. Therefore, local governments propose a systematic incen -
tive program to incentivize the green transformation of the industry. All 
fishing enterprises in the industry are required to jointly contribute to 
the formation of a peer-to-peer incentive pool of funds. Then, the local 
governments take over the pool of funds. They distribute these pool 
funds alongside official subsidies to enterprises that engage in green 
farming according to their actual situation. In addition, financial in -
stitutions promote enterprise green farming through the implementa -
tion of green credit. However, polluting enterprises frequently rely on 
outdated mariculture methods. These methods harm marine ecology, 
such as intensive mariculture, excessive antibiotic use, irrational feed 
management, and the direct discharge of pollutants into the sea. These 
practices are clearly contrary to the goals of green credit. Therefore, it is 
difficult to obtain green credit support for polluting enterprises. From 
the perspective of corporate social responsibility and peer incentives, 
non-polluting enterprises can reach a balanced interest relationship with 
polluting enterprises in order to maximize personal and social welfare. 
Specifically, non-polluting enterprises may leverage their own credit to 
assist polluting enterprises in implementing green farming through 
“ cost-sharing and benefit-sharing ” contracts. These agreements serve as 
credit guarantees, facilitating approach to green financing for polluting 
enterprises.
Peer incentives are structured on both practical and theoretical 
foundations. Practically, the mechanism originates from the Paris 
Agreement of 2016, which reflects a consensus that developed countries 
as major gas emitters, should assume greater responsibility and provide 
financial assistance to developing countries. Theoretically, Yang et al. 
(2018) validate the effectiveness of intra-organizational incentives 
through the framework of the public goods game.

The analysis above reveals a conflictual relationship between green

7. Case study
Case studies help to extract the characteristics and patterns of com -
plex phenomena, capturing and addressing emerging issues in man -
agement practices. To this end, this paper uses the case of promoting the 
construction of the “ Blue Granary ” in Putuo District, Zhoushan City, 
Zhejiang Province, to explore and verify the feasibility of the peer 
incentive policy proposed in this study.
Putuo District, Zhoushan City, Zhejiang Province, is located in the 
core area of the East China Sea fishing grounds, with abundant marine 
resources and a long-standing fishing tradition. However, issues such as 
the decline of nearshore fisheries resources, increasing environmental 
pollution, and the inefficiency of traditional aquaculture models have 
become increasingly prominent. To address these challenges, the Putuo 
District government has set the goal of constructing the “ Blue Granary, ” 
actively exploring pathways for the transformation to green aquacul -
ture, and becoming a demonstration zone for the green development of 
China ’ s marine aquaculture industry.
Putuo District took the lead in attempting the government-enterprise 
joint investment ” model. Specifically, this model involves the govern -
ment guiding enterprises to jointly contribute funds, establishing a 
dedicated fund pool to support green technology research and the 
ecological transformation of fish farms. As a result, Putuo District 
gradually established an incentive mechanism centered on the 
government-managed fund + targeted disbursement, ” assisting the local 
marine aquaculture industry in achieving green transformation. Over 
the past five years, the government leveraged 80 million yuan of fiscal 
funds to attract over 160 million yuan from enterprises, driving the 
implementation of five key projects, including the intelligent factory- 
based farming of king crabs, ecological farming of swimming crabs, 
and green farming of shallow-sea shellfish and algae, resulting in an 
annual output value exceeding 210 million yuan. Additionally, the local 
government has prioritized financial security, collaborating with large 
marine ranching enterprises to implement the fisheries mutual insur -
ance financial subsidy policy for aquaculture entities, and introducing 
the “ Fishing Guarantee Loan, ” which exempts counter-guarantees, to 
assist local aquaculturists and cooperatives, as well as other vulnerable 
aquaculture entities, in securing financing. In the past two years, this 
policy has provided loan support of 31.8 million yuan to several enter -
prises, significantly reducing the financing risks for vulnerable aqua -
culture entities.
It is evident that the government of Putuo District, Zhoushan City, 
Zhejiang Province, has not only alleviated fiscal pressure through 
government-enterprise joint investment and financial guarantees, but 
also provided credit support to aquaculture enterprises facing diffi -
culties in securing loans, effectively addressing the financing challenges. 
This provides practical evidence for the peer incentive policy proposed 
in this paper, validating its feasibility.
8. Conclusions and implications
8.1. Conclusions
This paper addresses two practical issues: limited government fiscal 
subsidies and high loan thresholds for polluting enterprises, by con -
structing a peer incentive mechanism. From a theoretical perspective, 
this paper explores the necessary conditions for the green trans -
formation of the marine aquaculture industry under this mechanism, 
and uses numerical simulation methods to conduct policy simulations. 
Subsequently, this paper also systematically analyzes the impact of 
several potential factors on the effectiveness of policy implementation. 
The results of the study are as follows: 
(1) There is a conflict relationship between environmental regulation 
and green credit. Existing literature generally focuses on the in -
dependent roles of environmental regulation and green credit in 
the green transformation of the marine aquaculture industry 
( Osmundsen et al., 2022 ; Xu and Zhu, 2024 ), with limited 
exploration of their synergistic effects. This paper, based on 
evolutionary game theory, uses numerical simulation methods to 
explore the synergistic effects of environmental regulation and 
green credit in the green transformation of the marine aquacul -
ture industry. The study shows that under the premise that 
market interest rates are variable, as green credit interest rates 
rise, the conflict between environmental regulation and green 
credit will significantly intensify, thereby affecting the green 
aquaculture returns of GFEs and TFEs. Therefore, to mitigate the 
negative effects of this conflict, the LGs need to increase the 
subsidy intensity. However, the increase in subsidies undoubt -
edly increases the LGs ’ fiscal pressure. This finding expands the 
current understanding of the relationship between environmental 
regulation and green credit, providing a theoretical basis for the 
formulation of more refined policy solutions.

(2) Peer incentive policy can not only substitute for environmental 
regulation, but also mitigate the loss of conflict between envi -
ronmental regulation and green credit, thereby reducing the 
financial pressure in the green transformation of the marine 
aquaculture industry. Existing literature on addressing the 
financial pressures of industrial green transformation primarily 
focuses on incentive measures such as “tax incentives” ( Boubaker 
et al., 2024 ), “government subsidies” ( Wu et al., 2022 ; Wan et al., 
2023 ), and “green credit” ( Shan et al., 2023 ; Thompson, 2022 ). 
However, these traditional financial support policies generally 
face challenges such as heavy fiscal burdens, limited funding 
supply, and short policy durations. To address this, this paper 
proposes a non-punitive green incentive mechanism with multi -
ple funding channels” – the peer incentive policy – based on both 
theoretical and practical foundations, and applies this policy to 
the marine aquaculture industry. The study indicates that the 
peer incentive policy not only can be a complement to incentive- 
based environmental regulation, but also more efficient than 
incentive-based environmental regulation in promoting green 
mariculture. In addition, peer incentive policy can give play the 
green credit incentive effect to reduce government regulatory 
pressure.
(3) Both of the allocation ratio of peer incentive levy and the effi -
ciency of monitoring of GFEs to TFEs can regulate the synergistic 
effect among environmental regulation, green credit and peer 
incentives. To further optimize the role of the peer incentive 
policy in the green transformation of the marine aquaculture 
industry, this paper uses numerical simulation methods to adjust 
the relevant parameters. The results show that, on the one hand, 
peer incentive policy can better mitigate the loss of implementing 
the environmental regulation and green credit under the condi -
tion of a higher proportion of levies on GFEs and a lower pro -
portion of levies on TFEs. Conversely, if the collection ratios are 
set improperly, this loss effect will be exacerbated. On the other 
hand, the increased monitoring efficiency of GFEs to TFEs can 
also reduce the loss of implementing the environmental regula -
tion and green credit. These findings not only clarify the key 
regulatory factors in the implementation of the peer incentive 
policy but also provide a quantitative basis for optimizing policy 
parameters, contributing to enhancing the precision and effec -
tiveness of policy implementation.
8.2. Implications
Based on the findings of the study, this paper makes the following 
implications. 
(1) The LGs should strengthen the implementation of incentive-based 
environmental regulatory policies to motivate fisheries enter -
prises to gain a competitive advantage in the market by choosing 
green mariculture. On the one hand, the environmental tax sys -
tem should be optimized to reduce the tax burden on GFEs. The 
government may implement favorable environmental tax rates to 
reduce the operating costs and green credit burdens for the 
fishing enterprises to conduct green aquaculture projects. In this 
way, a win-win situation in terms of both economic and envi -
ronmental benefits can be achieved through incentivizing fish -
eries enterprises to adopt more environmentally friendly 
technologies and cleaner aquaculture methods. On the other 
hand, the LGs may expand the sources of government subsidy 
funds. The sources of government subsidy funds may be enriched 
by issuing green bonds and setting up green investment fund. At 
the same time, the LGs can also adopt certain measures to attract 
and encourage private and international capital to invest in green 
aquaculture, so as to accelerate the process of green trans -
formation of the mariculture industry.
(2) Green equity and the differentiated credit policies can be imple -
mented to reduce financial risks, so as to support the develop -
ment of green mariculture. On the one hand, fishing enterprises 
can make good use of green equity to supplement their repayment 
sources. They can actively explore paths to convert their effec -
tiveness in ecological protection into economic value to supple -
ment additional sources of repayment, such as carbon emissions 
trading, sewage treatment fee rights, sewage rights. In this way, 
the gain from the green equity could be a part of the funding 
sources of repayment. Consequently, the loan risks can be 
reduced for the FIs at the same time. On the other hand, differ -
entiated credit policies could be implemented. For green credit 
projects undertaken by large and medium-sized fishery enter -
prises, FIs could grant preferential policies. These policies may 
include loan access, interest rate pricing, loan term, and guar -
antee methods, provided FIs remain financially sustainable. This 
approach aims to support the sustainable mariculture 
development.
(3) The LGs could optimize the use of green credit resources by 
improving the precision of green credit policies and expanding 
support of small and medium-sized enterprises. On the one hand, 
the targeting accuracy of green credit policies could be improved. 
The government and enterprises could collaborate to build a 
digital information-sharing platform to realize transparent 
sharing of policy guidance and scientific achievement on green 
aquaculture. This platform not only facilitates the supervision of 
lenders’ fisheries enterprises but also strengthen the govern -
ment’s oversight on the marine mariculture industry. In this way, 
the space for enterprises to seek rent could be limited, so as to 
improve the efficiency of the use of green credit funds. On the 
other hand, the green credit policy may be moderated to be in 
favor of TFEs. The government could provide credit endorsement 
for TFEs with targeted procurement to reduce the credit risk. In 
this way, the difficulties of financing of TFEs could be relieved. 
This approach not only ensures that the nature of green credit 
remains unchanged as well as the standard impaired but also 
expands the proportion of green credit granted to TFEs.
(4) The government may implement a peer incentive policy to 
accelerate the green transformation of the mariculture industry. 
In this paper, the positive effect of peer incentive mechanism on 
the green transformation of mariculture industry is verified better 
than environmental regulation policies. Therefore, the LGs could 
establish a peer incentive fund pool to incentive fishing enter -
prises to engage in green mariculture. The peer incentive fund 
pool can be funded by multiple entities and from multiple sour -
ces, such as peer incentive levies, environmental penalty levies, 
and public benefit donations. In addition, a differentiated levy 
rate for peer incentives can be implemented. For fishing enter -
prises with poor environmental performance, the collection ratio 
could be higher to force them to carry out green transformation. 
While for fishing enterprises with good environmental perfor -
mance, the collection ratio could be lower to reward them for 
their great effort to carry out green transformation and adopt 
clean mariculture technologies.
8.3. Limitations
Based on theoretical and practical foundations, this paper proposes 
the peer incentive mechanism as an effective supplement in the green 
transformation of the marine aquaculture industry, addressing issues 
such as limited government fiscal subsidies and high loan thresholds for 
polluting enterprises. This paper also explores its advantages in allevi -
ating financial pressure and promoting the synergistic effects of green 
credit and environmental regulation. However, despite providing a new 
theoretical framework and policy recommendations, the following 
limitations still exist: Firstly, the model assumptions in this paper are

based on a series of idealized conditions, which may not fully account 
for the complexities in real-world operations. For instance, the actual 
implementation of the policy may be influenced by other stakeholders, 
such as the central government, environmental agencies, and coastal 
communities, which are not represented in the model. Secondly, this 
paper primarily focuses on theoretical analysis and numerical simula -
tion results, lacking sufficient empirical data support. Although case 
study is used to attempt to validate the policy effects, due to the lack of 
extensive cross-regional and cross-industry empirical studies, it remains 
difficult to fully assess the application effects and long-term impacts of 
the peer incentive mechanism in different contexts. Finally, the imple -
mentation of the peer incentive mechanism still faces practical chal -
lenges. On one hand, due to the existence of different types of fishing 
enterprises within the industry, especially the interest differences be -
tween GFEs and TFEs, GFEs may be unwilling to bear the higher coop -
eration costs, while TFEs may lack sufficient motivation for green 
transformation, thus hindering the effective promotion of the peer 
incentive mechanism. On the other hand, issues such as cost problems 
during policy implementation, regulatory efficiency, and the reasonable 
collection and distribution ratio of incentive funds may all affect the 
effectiveness of policy implementation. If the policy design is improper, 
it may lead to resource wastage or a weakening of the incentive effects, 
which could affect the overall outcome of the green transformation of 
the marine aquaculture industry.
